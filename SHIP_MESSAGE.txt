THE MODULE. A deep-well HPHT simulator (TD ~20,300 ft) with a quartz P/T
gauge string whose drift is suppressed by the canonical lattice:

  suppression = (0.58+0.32*(1-F_TRZ)) * (0.52+0.38*K_MEX) * (0.68+0.27*Phi_res)
              = 1.0324 at the locked {0.1, 25/12, 0.84}

- drift BELOW the 0.215 %FS/yr industry baseline (0.336 %FS/yr at the HPHT
flagship 6,200 m / 205 C / 18,500 psi, verified live).

THE KNOB RULING (Rule 2). The template's adjustable "K_MEX" (1.15) and
"Phi_res" (0.93) sliders were tuning gains wearing primitive names. The
canonical values are LOCKED inside the physics (the dispatch re-verifies the
lock on every call); the sliders are renamed k_structural_trim /
phi_coupling_trim - honest engineering instrument gains. The Qt6 GUI shows
the locked canonicals read-only.

v1.1.0 EXTENSIONS (Daniel-directed, same session):
1. N-gauge strings - make_sensor_string(n); verified at 12 gauges.
2. Real well profiles from CSV - load_well_profile_csv (depth_ft,
   pressure_psi, temp_F); the shipped sample carries an HPHT overpressure
   kick below 16,500 ft that the linear model CANNOT see (9,313 psi) and the
   profile CAPTURES (18,405 psi at the deepest gauge).
3. Twin-gauge drift-comparison mode - every station carries a conventional
   reference gauge (same baseline + stress, suppression = 1). Away from the
   clip band the measured conventional/UQFF ratio EQUALS the canonical
   suppression: 1.0326 vs 1.0324 single-point; 1.0325 mean across a 12-gauge
   string on the real profile. The PAPER_2250 quartz bench test now has its
   complete simulation instrument, with comparison columns in the CSV export.
