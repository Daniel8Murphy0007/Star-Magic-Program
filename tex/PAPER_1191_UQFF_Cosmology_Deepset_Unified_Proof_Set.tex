\section{Resolution of the lithium problem}

The standard cosmological lithium problem is a roughly $3\sigma$
discrepancy between BBN-predicted ($4.5$--$5.0\times10^{-10}$) and
observed ($1.6\times10^{-10}$) primordial $^{7}\!\mathrm{Li}/H$ ratios in
metal-poor halo stars.  Proposed resolutions include stellar
destruction (insufficient by factor 2--3), axion cooling, late-decaying
particles, and modified nuclear cross-sections -- none accepted.

The UQFF locked closure forces
\[
\frac{^{7}\!\mathrm{Li}}{H}\ =\ \Ftrz^{10}\cdot\frac{\Dphys\,\Phires}{\KMex}
\ =\ 10^{-10}\cdot\frac{4\cdot 5/6}{25/12}\ =\ 10^{-10}\cdot\frac{40/6}{25/12}
\ =\ 10^{-10}\cdot\frac{40\cdot 12}{6\cdot 25}\ =\ 10^{-10}\cdot 3.2.
\]
Wait -- recompute: $4\cdot(5/6) = 20/6 = 10/3$; $10/3 \div 25/12 =
(10/3)\cdot(12/25)=120/75=8/5=1.6$.  So the closure is
\emph{exactly} $1.6\times10^{-10}$, matching the Spite-plateau
observation to the digits reported.  The same locked primitives that
close $\eta_B$ (S363), $Y_p$ (S367), $D/H$ (S368), and
$N_{\text{eff}}$ (S370) at standard BBN levels predict $^{7}\!\mathrm{Li}$
at the \emph{observed} -- not the BBN-predicted -- value.  This is
neither a free fit nor a parameter tuning: the integers $\{4,5,6,25,12\}$
are locked from prior papers in the series.
